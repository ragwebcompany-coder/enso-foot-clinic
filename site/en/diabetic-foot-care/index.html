Diabetic Foot Care

Diabetes is a chronic condition and, over time, it can lead to complications. Where the feet are concerned, the two most important are peripheral neuropathy and peripheral vascular disease. The first reduces sensation, the second reduces blood supply. Together they create a foot that can be injured without the patient noticing, and that struggles to heal.

Why the diabetic foot is different

In a foot with normal sensation, pain is an alarm system: a tight shoe, a small stone, a blister — all noticed within minutes and corrected. In diabetic neuropathy that alarm is silent. A patient can walk for hours on an open wound without knowing it.

The problem with the diabetic foot is not that it hurts. It is that it doesn't.

The assessment

Foot screening should be carried out at least once a year for every person with diabetes, and more often if your physician or podologist advises it. It follows a structured protocol:

  1. Protective sensation — 10 g monofilament at defined sites, plus 128 Hz tuning fork for vibration sense.
  2. Circulation — dorsalis pedis and posterior tibial pulses, skin colour and temperature, capillary refill.
  3. Skin and nails — mapping of callus, fissures, fungal infection and interdigital moisture. Callus on an insensate foot is a pre-ulcerative sign.
  4. Deformity — claw toes, hallux valgus, collapsed arch. All of them move load onto areas not built to carry it.
  5. Footwear — we inspect the shoes you arrived in: internal seams, worn soles, wrong size, removable insole.

You leave with a risk category, a written daily care plan and specific footwear advice.

What to do every day

  • Inspect the soles every evening — including under the toes, with a mirror if needed.
  • Wash with lukewarm water, never hot: your sensation is not a reliable thermostat.
  • Dry carefully, especially between the toes.
  • Moisturise sole and heel — not between the toes.
  • Cut nails straight across. Do not round the corners.
  • Never walk barefoot, not even indoors.
  • Check inside your shoes with your hand before putting them on.
  • Never cut corns yourself and never use acid corn plasters.
