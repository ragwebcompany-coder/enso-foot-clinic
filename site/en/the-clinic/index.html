The Clinic

An ensō is the circle drawn in a single stroke — complete and open at the same time. It is a fair description of the work here: a thorough job on the foot that does not close the conversation but starts it.

The clinic is in Peraia, Thessaloniki, and continues a practice that began in 2002. It is run by Elpiniki Christodoulou, a podologist with more than 19 years in foot therapy and foot aesthetics.

How we work

Every appointment starts with the same question: why here and not somewhere else. A callus under the third metatarsal, a fissure on the right heel only, a nail that always ingrows on the same side — none of that is random. Each one is the imprint of a pressure, a shoe, a way of walking.

Treat only the symptom and we will see you again in six weeks. Solve the pressure and we will see you in six months. That is the goal.

Our principles

  • Sterilisation without shortcuts. Cleaning, disinfection and class-B autoclave sterilisation, in sealed pouches opened in front of you. Anything that cannot be sterilised is single-use.
  • Dry technique. No soaking, no shared basins. Working on dry skin is more precise, more controlled and safer.
  • Painless. What we remove is dead tissue. We make no incisions, give no injections and perform no surgery.
  • A written plan. You leave knowing what to do at home, what to change about your footwear, and when to come back.
  • Stated limits. When a case belongs to a dermatologist, diabetologist, vascular or orthopaedic surgeon, we say so and refer you.

What we treat

  • Diabetic foot care — annual assessment and prevention of ulceration.
  • Calluses and corns — removal plus offloading, so they do not come back.
  • Dry and cracked heels — debridement first, hydration second.
  • Ingrown toenail — painless correction with a glass-fibre brace.
  • Medical pedicure — the clinic's core session.

Practical details

The clinic is at Romanou 11, Peraia, Thessaloniki, 3rd floor. Appointments in Athens are also available by phone. Consultations can be held in English.

Bring the shoes you actually wear every day — not your best pair. It is the single most useful piece of information you can give us.
